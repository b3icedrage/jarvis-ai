🎓 You just watched a scam in action

Nothing about this was real — no website was checked, no fee existed, and no M-Pesa prompt was sent. The whole sequence was engineered to make you pay before you could verify anything.

Scam mechanics — what’s really happening

  • 🚩 Urgency & pressure: “pay today or your project is deleted”
  • 🚩 A manufactured “website not found” error after a correct submission
  • 🚩 A surprise “activation/launch fee” from a personal number
  • 🚩 Only one phone number is accepted — everyone else is rejected

Spot the red flags

In the real scam, at least one of these appears in every message. If you see them — stop and verify.

Surprise fees

Legitimate schools and platforms don’t demand sudden “launch”, “activation” or “recovery” fees through personal numbers.

Manufactured errors

“Your website was not found / not uploaded” — after you submitted it correctly. The error is invented to create panic.

Personal M-Pesa number

Payments are demanded to a personal phone, not an official paybill or till number belonging to the school.

Urgency & pressure

“Do it now”, “before midnight”, “or your work will be deleted” — pressure exists so you don’t ask questions or verify.

Unofficial channels

Contact comes from personal numbers or WhatsApp, not the school’s official phone, email, or office.

“I can’t see it” claims

Claims that don’t match how the school actually works. Real staff can show you records and use official systems.

If this happens to you

The scam only works if you pay before verifying. This is your playbook.

STEP 1

Don’t pay

No legitimate process needs a surprise “launch fee”. Any payment is money straight to the scammer.

STEP 2

Verify with the school

Contact the school office, your teacher, or the official phone number you already know — never the one in the message.

STEP 3

Keep the evidence

Screenshot every message, the phone number, and the M-Pesa request. It’s useful for reporting.

STEP 4

Report it

Tell a teacher or parent. In Kenya, report fraud to the police/DCI (e.g. hotline 0800 722 203) and report the number to Safaricom.

STEP 5

Warn others

Share the red flags with classmates. Scammers rely on each student being contacted in isolation.
